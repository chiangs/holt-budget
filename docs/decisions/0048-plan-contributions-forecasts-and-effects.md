## Shape
Three new fact tables, each with a branded ID, and one new field.

- **`people`.** `{ id: PersonId; name: string; kind: "adult" | "child"; birthMonth?: Month }`. A birth month is for a child and is used by the children's timeline later.
- **`contributionAgreements`.** `{ id: AgreementId; personId: PersonId; accountId: AccountId; amount: Money; day: number; from: Month; to?: Month }`. The person is an adult and the account is shared. The amount is above zero. `day` is 1 to 28, the day the money usually lands. `from` and `to` are the first and last months it funds, so a change of amount is a new agreement from a later month, which keeps the history.
- **`bucketTransfers`.** `{ id: BucketTransferId; month: Month; from: JobId; to: JobId; amount: Money; reason: string }`. Both jobs keep their money and differ, the amount is above zero and the month is open, reopened or closed. A transfer out of the buffer needs a reason (the buffer's withdrawal, in the domain model).
- **`Treatment` income gains `agreement?: AgreementId`**, allowed only when the source is "contribution". This is what ties a transaction to who paid it.

## Definitions
- **Buckets include transfers.** `bucketEnd(j, m) = bucketEnd(j, m−1) + assigned(j, m) + transfersIn(j, m) − transfersOut(j, m) − spent(j, m)`. A transfer between jobs in different accounts needs a move at the bank, and until it is made the account's remainder is flagged (0046, rule 6). The screen creates both in one batch.
- **Contribution status.** For an agreement and a funds month `m`, money is expected on day `day` of month `m−1` (decision 1). Take the income transactions linked to the agreement whose funds month is `m`. The status is the first of these that applies:
  1. `arrived`: they total the amount or more, from one transaction, booked no later than three days after the expected date;
  2. `late`: the same, booked later;
  3. `parts`: they total the amount or more, from two or more transactions;
  4. `short`: they total more than zero and less than the amount;
  5. `overdue`: nothing arrived and the date is more than three days past the expected day;
  6. `expected`: nothing arrived yet.

  It is a record, not a judgement (domain model), so the wording is neutral.
- **Expected income** for a month is each active agreement's amount less what arrived, for the statuses `expected`, `overdue` and `short`. It is never part of money available, which counts only what arrived (rule 4).
- **Monthly moves count expected contributions** as money landing in their account. This answers 0046's second open question: before payday, the account the contributions land in no longer shows the whole month's assignments as a move in.
- **Savings rate** for a month is saving ÷ money in, as tenths of a percent (so 9.9% is 99), or null when no money is in. Saving is the month's assigned amount for every job in a group that counts towards the savings rate, and for every pay-into job. Money in is the income that funds the month, plus the expected income.
- **Goal.** For a save-up job: its target (the amount for "amount by month", the buffer months times the essential monthly total for the buffer, none for a plain monthly amount), what the bucket holds, the monthly amount, and the months to go, `ceil((target − saved) ÷ monthly)`. It is 0 when reached and null when there is no target or no monthly amount.
- **Forecast** for a job that keeps its money: for each month from the current one to the horizon, the planned amount (the assignment, or the projection), the estimated bill, and the bucket's end after both, with the lowest point and the first month it would go below zero. It answers 0046's first open question.
- **Starting to even out.** For a followed-the-bills job: the monthly amount from the next 12 months' estimates (`evenedMonthlyAmount`), then two offers. *Start now* gives the smallest seed that keeps the bucket at or above zero over the 12 months, with a margin of one even-out step (`minimumSeed`), and the buffer's runway after taking it. *Start later* gives the earliest month from which evening out never takes the bucket below zero with no seed.
- **Moves at the bank for a month.** The surplus accounts (money to move out) are paired with the accounts that need money in, largest first, as concrete moves, next to the one-off moves already stored. Each has a status: `done` when imported transfer pairs between the two accounts, booked from the 15th of the month before to the end of the month, total the amount or more; `partlyDone` when they total some of it; otherwise `toDo`; `skipped` for a one-off move the family skipped.
- **Where to take money from.** For a new job needing more than is unassigned, the options are one split across the non-essential jobs that have money assigned (even shares, the remainder to the first) and one option for each of those jobs alone. An essential job is never offered.
- **The effects of a command.** `previewEffects(facts, command, { asOf })` applies the command, works out the figures before and after, and returns the month's changes: unassigned or over-assigned, each job whose assigned amount changes, the savings rate, each goal whose months to go change, and the buffer's runway. It is pure and writes nothing. A refusal is returned as it is.

## Found while building the tables
The three tables, the income field, the bucket maths and the storage are built (Milestone 4, first step). The code does what this says, and a test pins it.

7. **One list of faults per kind of row.** `agreementFaults` and `transferFaults` return each thing wrong with a row as a violation code and as a refusal code. `checkFacts` flags the violations on stored facts, and the command handlers refuse with the first refusal, so what a command refuses and what the check finds cannot disagree. `checkFacts` also keeps its usual rule that a command is refused only for violations it adds.
8. **Storage.** Migration `0003_people_contributions_transfers.sql` adds `people`, `contribution_agreements` and `bucket_transfers`. It reshapes no table, so no stored change record is rewritten or deleted, and a test migrates a version 2 database that holds a record and undoes it. The columns that are SQL keywords are `from_month` and `to_month` (agreements) and `from_job` and `to_job` (transfers). The database checks the kind of a person, positive amounts, a day from 1 to 28, the format of every month, an agreement's end not before its start, and a transfer between two different jobs. Every foreign key is deferrable and initially deferred, and every child foreign key column leads an index (finding 15 of 0047). The income `agreement` lives inside the treatment JSON, which the database cannot check, so only `checkFacts` and the row schema do.
9. **A transfer needs a month with a budget month row.** The command accepts a closed month as well as an open or reopened one, as the shape above says. Taking money out in a closed month changes the buckets from that month on and no assignment, so it needs no reopening. This is an interpretation for Stephen to confirm.
10. **`moveBucket` checks the bucket at the end of the transfer's month**, with every earlier transfer, assignment and bit of spending in the facts. It does not look at later months, so a transfer can leave a later month short, as lowering an assignment can. The check works out the figures as of today, as `openMonth` and `deleteJob` do.
11. **A transfer changes the plan of a planned month for one kind of job.** A save-up job with a target date projects its monthly amount from what its bucket holds, so moving money into it lowers the amount projected in every planned month, and so changes what is unassigned there. A month the family has opened keeps its assignments, so nothing changes in it. The properties that say a transfer changes no assignment, unassigned money or balance hold for any other job, and a test shows the save-up case with numbers.
12. **`deletePerson` is a command that the shape above did not name.** It is refused while any agreement names the person, an ended agreement included. Agreements are never deleted, because an agreement ends by setting its last month, so a person who has had an agreement stays. This is a product call for Stephen: the alternative is a `deleteAgreement` command that the screen offers only for a mistake.
13. **A job a transfer names cannot be deleted.** It gets its own refusal, `jobHasTransfers`, because `jobInUse` says "assignments or transactions" and would mislead. `changeJob` that stops a named job keeping its money is refused as `breaksFacts` with the violation `transferJobKeepsNoMoney`, with no plainer message yet.
14. **Twelve refusal codes are new**: `personNotFound`, `personInUse`, `agreementNotFound`, `personNotAdult`, `accountNotShared`, `dayOutOfRange`, `agreementEndsBeforeStart`, `transferMonthNotStarted`, `bucketNotKept`, `bufferMoveNeedsReason`, `bucketHoldsLess` and `jobHasTransfers`. Each needs a `refusal.<code>` entry in the renderer's message files with core's text, which `routes/transactions/refusal.test.ts` checks.
15. **`JobMonth` gains `transfersIn` and `transfersOut`**, and a bucket's `start` and `end` follow the formula above. `unassigned` and `available` read neither, and they stay as they were.
16. **The generators reach the new commands.** `arbitraryScript` starts with people and adds six steps (`addPerson`, `changePerson`, `deletePerson`, `addAgreement`, `changeAgreement`, `moveBucket`). Over 600 random scripts it accepts about 440 agreements, 200 changes of agreements and 250 transfers. `arbitraryFacts` fills the three tables, an end on some agreements, and the agreement on some income. `arbitraryHousehold` is unchanged, so the older properties see the same households.

## Found while building the contributions
The statuses, expected income, money in, the monthly moves and the needs-you suggestion are built in `packages/core/src/figures/contributions.ts`. The code does what this says, and a test pins it. The items are numbered C1 and on so that they stay apart from the other lists of this record.

C1. **Three pure functions, not part of `Figures`.** `contributionStatuses(facts, asOf)`, `expectedIncome(facts, asOf, horizonMonths = 12)` and `moneyIn(facts, asOf, horizonMonths = 12)`. The screen calls them with the facts it holds. `computeFigures` uses `expectedIncome` for one thing only, the monthly moves, and `accountFigures` takes the result as an argument.
C2. **Which months each covers.** A status exists for each agreement and each month it funds from two months before the current month to one after it, and never before the plan starts. The money that arrives in a month funds the next, so "one after" is the month this month's payday funds. Expected income and money in cover the current month to the horizon. A month that has ended has no expected income, so an overdue contribution for a month already over stays visible as a status and no longer changes the account's move.
C3. **Parts are counted by transactions.** Two or more linked transactions that reach the amount are `parts`, even when one alone would have reached it. A part carries no lateness, so a second payment after the third day does not turn the month `late`. `short` is any total above zero and below the amount, however long ago it came.
C4. **A linked refund lowers what arrived.** The total is net, so a linked payment of minus 5,000 kr after a payment of 20,000 kr gives `short` with 5,000 kr still expected. Expected income is `max(0, amount − arrived)` for expected, overdue and short, and zero for arrived, late and parts. So for a month, what arrived plus what is expected is the larger of the amount and what arrived.
C5. **A contribution nobody has linked is money in the account, and it is not a payment.** It counts as income that funds its month (so `moneyIn().actual` and the account's monthly move include it), and it counts toward no agreement, so until the family links it the month also still expects the agreement's amount and shows twice. The needs-you suggestion below is how it gets linked. This is exactly as the brief says, and it is a product call for Stephen whether the screen should say so ("a contribution in Everyday has no agreement").
C6. **Monthly moves.** An account's inbound for a month from the current one is the income that has arrived there plus the expected income of the agreements that pay into it. Only an agreement's own account counts, whichever account the payment actually landed in. Months before the current one are unchanged. The shortfall flag's `monthlyMove` follows. Money available, unassigned and carried-in are unchanged, and two tests (one on a hand-made household and one property over generated ones) prove it.
C7. **The needs-you suggestion.** A new kind, `contribution`, with the agreement, the person's name and a `setTreatment` to income for the month after the booking month with the agreement set. It ranks below a rule and a merge and above same as last time. A waiting transaction of money in, in the agreement's account, that covers the month it would fund and is not before the plan start, is matched when its booking day is within 7 days of the agreement's day (the money funds the month after its booking month, so the expected day is a day of the booking month) and when its amount is above zero and at most what the agreement still waits for. That is the whole amount while nothing has arrived. The amount that is exactly what is left fits better than a part of it, then the nearer day wins, and a tie gives no suggestion. A month already paid in full has nothing left to match.
C8. **Two readings for Stephen to confirm.** An amount matches when it is at most what the agreement still waits for, so the payment that completes a short month is matched and a full payment on top of a part is not. The stricter reading, "the whole amount, or less than what is left", would miss the payment that completes a month. And a payment booked early in a month for a day late in the previous month (paid on 28 November, booked on 1 December) is not matched, because decision 1 funds the month after the booking month and so its expected day would be in December.
C9. **The suggestion reads every linked payment, not those booked by today.** `needsYouItems(facts)` has no `asOf`. The statuses count only what is booked by today, and the two differ only for a payment booked in the future, which an import does not make.

## Found while building the effects
The goals, the savings rate, where to take money from, the effects of a command and the moves at the bank are built in `packages/core/src/figures/{goals,savings-rate,moves-plan}.ts`, `funding.ts` and `effects.ts`. The code does what this says, and a test pins it. The numbers in the tests are the Plan mockup's November (`testing/plan-household.ts`).

E1. **Functions of the facts, not part of `Figures`.** `goalOf`, `savingsRate`, `fundingOptions` and `movesPlan` take the facts and `asOf`, and each has a `From` variant (`goalsFrom`, `savingsRatesFrom`, `fundingOptionsFrom`, `movesPlanFrom`) that takes figures the screen already holds, so a screen that has `useFigures` pays for no second pass. `previewEffects` works the figures out twice, covering the plan only up to the current month, because it reports only that month. On the family's volume (8,556 transactions, 40 jobs) a preview of a batch of two commands takes a median of 9 ms, of which `apply` is 2.9 ms and a pass 1.9 ms; the 95th percentile was 14 to 19 ms on a machine busy with other work. `testing/measure-effects.ts` measures it.
E2. **A goal's monthly amount is what the job is given in the current month**, and what it has saved is its bucket at the end of that month, transfers included. The months to go follow the order no target (null), target reached (0, even when nothing is given), nothing given (null), otherwise `ceil((target - saved) / monthly)`. The ready month is the current month plus the months to go. A buffer with no essential spending has no target, because three months of nothing is no goal. For a job with a date, `reachBy` says what each month from next month to the date must give, from what the bucket holds now ("3,000 kr a month for 7 months, December to June"). `monthlyToReach` is the same sum with no job, for a New job form, and a test shows it equals what the figures project for every planned month.
E3. **The mockup's "about 6 months later" does not follow from its numbers.** The buffer holds 86,000 of 158,460 kr and is given 3,000 kr a month, which is 25 months. After taking 1,386 kr from it, it holds 84,614 kr and is given 1,614 kr, which is 46 months, 21 months later. The screen shows the computed months.
E4. **The savings rate rounds to the nearest tenth of a percent, a half up**, by integer arithmetic. The mockup's 14.0% and 9.9% are 13.96% and 9.89%, so rounding down would show 13.9% and 9.8%. Money in is the income that funds the month plus an optional `expectedIncome(facts, month)`. It takes the facts because a preview works it out for the facts before and the facts after a change, and a change can add an agreement. The screen wraps `expectedIncome(facts, asOf)` from the contributions. A rate can pass 100% when the plan saves more than comes in.
E5. **Where to take money from.** The money with no job goes first. The jobs that can give are the active, non-essential jobs with money assigned that month, less any the caller excludes, largest assignment first and the ID breaking a tie. The split gives each an even share and the remainder to the first. A job that cannot take its share gives all it has, and the others share the difference. The split is offered only when two or more jobs give, and a single option only for a job that covers the rest alone. A month with money to spare offers one option that moves nothing, an over-assigned month has no money with no job, and when the jobs cannot cover the rest there are no options and `shortfall` says how much is missing. A test shows that no option takes from an essential, paused, finished or excluded job, takes more than a job has assigned, or misses the amount.
E6. **The commands.** `fundingCommands` assigns the money with no job first and then moves each share, so the caller's `addJob` and these go in one batch. `assign` sets an amount, so for a job that already has an assignment the caller passes it as `alreadyAssigned`, or the money already there is lost. Only an open or reopened month takes assignments: for a month the family has not opened the options exist and the commands are refused as `monthNotOpen`.
E7. **The effects are for the month of `asOf`** and are plain data. A job the command adds has 0 before and a job it removes has 0 after. A goal is listed when its months to go or its ready month change, or when the command adds or removes it (before or after is null). When a change of the plan start leaves today before the plan, there is no current month, nothing is unassigned and no job is listed. A property test found that case. A refusal comes back as `apply` gives it.
E8. **Moves at the bank.** The surplus accounts and the accounts that need money in are paired largest first, the ID breaking a tie, so the same facts give the same moves whatever order the rows are held in. `unmoved` lists what no move can cover: money with no job stays in the account the income lands in (the mockup's 228 kr), and an over-assigned month leaves a need. With every krone assigned nothing is unmoved and every account nets to zero. A monthly move counts the imported transfer pairs between its two accounts whose outgoing side is booked from the 15th of the month before to the end of the month and not after today, except a transfer that completed a one-off move, which belongs to that move. The windows of two months overlap by half a month, so a transfer made on 20 October counts for October and for November. For October a late transfer can then hide a missing move, which matters little once the month has gone. A one-off move shows the status stored, so an imported transfer completes it only through `completesMove`.

## Found while building the forecast
The forecast, the history, the estimate and the offers to even out are built in `packages/core/src/figures/{forecast,job-history,job-estimate,even-out-offer}.ts`. The code does what this says, and a test pins it.

F1. **Functions of the facts, not part of `Figures`.** `jobForecast`, `jobHistory`, `jobEstimate` and `evenOutOffer` each take `(facts, jobId, asOf)` and return plain data, or null for a job that doesn't exist or has nothing to say (a forecast needs a job that keeps its money, an estimate a spend job, an offer an active spend job). Each works out the figures once, about 2 ms on the synthetic year with 40 jobs (median 0.8 ms for the history, 2.1 to 2.5 ms for the others, where one `computeFigures` takes 2.2 ms). A screen that already holds figures calls `forecastFromFigures(figures, jobId)`. `startNowCommands(offer, { ids, reason })` is the one function that needs IDs, which the caller supplies in a fixed order: the transfer, then the move at the bank.
F2. **Twelve months, not thirteen.** The mockup's year runs November to October, the current month and the eleven after it, and its estimates sum to 36,300 kr. `computeFigures` reaches a thirteenth month (`current + 12`), which the forecast leaves out. A plan that hasn't started has no current month, so no forecast and no offer.
F3. **The current month's bill is the estimate, or what is already paid if that is more.** The figures add nothing for a bill that hasn't come, so a month ahead only grows. The forecast takes the bill out, and a test shows that its bucket differs from `JobFigures` by exactly the bills still to come, for any household.
F4. **The mockup's seed is 4,100 kr and the rule gives 4,000 kr.** The rule is `minimumSeed` with a margin of one even-out step, 50 kr: 3,950 kr to reach zero and 50 kr to spare, so the bucket is lowest in March at 50 kr and holds 4,300 kr in October. The mockup's 4,100 kr is the same rule with a margin of 150 kr, three steps, and shows 150 kr to spare and 4,400 kr. `minimumSeed` applies the margin only when a seed is needed, so a bucket that touches zero exactly needs no seed, and one øre below needs two steps (100 kr). Stephen decides whether the margin should be larger or should apply when the bucket would only just hold.
F5. **A job that keeps no money starts a bucket with every earlier leftover when it is evened out.** An evened-out job always keeps its money, so its bucket counts each month's leftover from the plan start, a past overspend as a negative. In the mockup, 2,900 kr planned in each of ten months against 28,800 kr paid leaves 200 kr, so the bucket starts at 200 kr and the seed is 3,800 kr, not 4,000 kr. That 200 kr was returned to unassigned and assigned elsewhere, so the bucket now counts money that other jobs hold. The offer shows what `apply` will give (`withoutSeed.startsWith` says what the bucket already holds). Counting an evened-out job's bucket only from its start month, and returning the leftovers before it as for any other job, would be a change to `figures/months.ts` and `keepsMoney`. It is a product and core call for Stephen.
F6. **The commands set the assignments of the open months the plan covers.** `changeJob` leaves an open month's assignment alone, so the offer also assigns each open or reopened month: the monthly amount from the start, the estimate before it (the mockup's "November is now 3,050 kr; the extra 150 kr came from the 228 kr with no job" and "November follows the estimate, 3,300 kr"). A closed month keeps its assignment, so the seed grows to cover it. A planned month needs nothing, because it projects the plan. The assignments are in the offer as `{ month, from, to }`.
F7. **Start now can be blocked, and says why.** The seed comes from the buffer when it holds at least that much at the end of this month. Otherwise `blockedBy` is `bufferHoldsLess`, `noBuffer`, or `monthNotStarted` when this month has no budget month to record the move in. The numbers stay, `startNowCommands` returns null, and a test applies the same plan and move to show that core refuses each. The batch is the plan, the assignments, the move between buckets and, when the buffer and the job are in different accounts, the move at the bank. The reason text comes from the caller, because the buffer needs one and the screen words it.
F8. **Start later is the earliest month, from the second one, that needs no seed.** It is absent when starting now needs no seed, because there is nothing to wait for, and absent when no month in the year gets by without one. Until the start the plan follows the bills, so the bucket stays where it is. A property test works out every earlier month through core's own commands and finds each one short.
F9. **The proposal for a job already evened out.** When the forecast runs below zero, the offer gives the smallest monthly amount, in whole steps, that keeps the bucket at or above zero, with the assignments and the command that would set it. It gives none when the dip comes before evening out starts (no monthly amount can fill it) or when the plan's own amount would hold and the dip is only an open month the family assigned less to. A month the family never opened projects the monthly amount too, so a higher amount also lifts the bucket that month starts with. The app never applies it by itself.
F10. **An estimate names its method and the months it used, and checks its own answer.** `estimate.ts` returns only the figure, the method and a rough flag, so `explainEstimate` names the months (the same month last year and the trend, the peak and its comparable months, or the last six) and works the estimate again from just those months. A basis that gives a different figure is returned as `unexplained`, never as a wrong description, and a property test over random histories never meets one. If `Estimate` carried the months it used, `explainEstimate` could go. The family's own figure for a month says so and keeps the figure the app would have given.
F11. **The history starts at the job's first month with a plan or a bill.** A job added in July doesn't have six empty months pulling its average down. Months before the plan start show what was paid and no plan. The totals cover the months that have ended ("Jan to Oct, actual 28,800 kr, 2,880 a month on average"), and the last three come with what was planned less what was paid, so the screen shows "184 kr under" without working it out.
F12. **The generator.** `arbitraryVariableCost` builds a household around one variable cost: a plan of each kind, a month in each state, history before and after the plan start, overrides, moves between buckets and the buffer, and some households with no buffer. The properties check that the offer's commands are accepted by `apply` and leave exactly the bucket the offer shows, that the seed keeps the lowest point between one and two steps above zero, and that no earlier month needs no seed. Twenty-four deliberate breaks each turn a test red.

For Stephen to confirm: the margin of one step and that it applies only when a seed is needed (F4); whether an evened-out job should count past leftovers (F5); that starting also assigns the open months (F6); and that the current month's bill is the larger of the estimate and what is paid (F3).
